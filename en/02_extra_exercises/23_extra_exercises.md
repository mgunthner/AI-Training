# Extra Exercises 23 – Everyday Workflows & Prompt Library

To the cheatsheet: `01_cheatsheets/23_everyday_workflows`

**Levels:** ★ Getting started · ★★ Everyday · ★★★ Challenge
**Rule:** Use only invented or anonymized data. All exercises work with any AI service and in the free plan. Suggested solutions are at the end. Only look after you're done.

## Exercise 1 ★ – Set up a library
**Scenario:** You don't have a prompt library yet.

**Task:** Create a note file (e.g. "my_prompts.md") with this structure:

```
# My Prompt Library

## [Title]
- Purpose:
- Prompt:
- Example result: (anonymized)
- Note: (model, mode, date)
```

Enter a first prompt that worked well for you during the training.

**Hint:** The structure matters more than the quantity – every entry needs a title, purpose, and prompt.

## Exercise 2 ★ – Choose a workflow
**Scenario:** The eight standard workflows from the cheatsheet (email, summary, learning plan, research, text revision, table help, decision preparation, meeting preparation).

**Task:** Pick the workflow that brings you the greatest benefit in everyday life and test it with invented data. Note: (a) What works well? (b) What do you adapt for yourself (length, tone, format)?

**Hint:** The best workflow is the one you really use again and again – not the most sophisticated one.

## Exercise 3 ★★ – Professionalize a vague prompt
**Scenario:** This poorly working prompt: "Make something useful out of this text."

**Task:** Turn it into a library-ready prompt for **text revision** with: goal, context, format, constraints, target audience. Test both versions with the same invented text and compare the results.

**Hint:** Use the template from cheatsheet 05: role/goal → context → task → format → limits.

---

## Exercise 4 ★★ – Apply the mini quality check
**Scenario:** You have an AI text in front of you (e.g. a club newsletter based on invented data).

**Task:** Check it with the mini quality check: (1) **Is it correct?** – cross-check facts and numbers. (2) **Am I allowed to share it?** – data, rights, labeling (topic 22). (3) **Is it my style?** – sound, length, you/formal address. For each check, write your result and at least one correction.

**Hint:** The check deliberately takes only a few minutes – it's a filter, not a final edit.

---

## Exercise 5 ★★★ – When AI, when not?
**Scenario:** Eight situations from your everyday life (invented versions):

1. A thank-you email to a colleague after a difficult phase
2. A weekly report with a fixed format
3. A sympathy card to someone close to you
4. A summary of a 40-page report for yourself
5. Research preparation for vacation planning
6. A parenting question that concerns your parents
7. A tip calculator for a group bill
8. An apology letter to a customer after a mistake

**Task:** Decide for each situation: **AI yes** (which workflow?), **AI with strong own review** or **AI no** (why?). Justify briefly.

**Hint:** Criteria from the cheatsheet: reliability, relationship, verifiability, repeatability.

## Suggested solutions

**Exercise 1:** No fixed result. What matters: the file exists and the first entry is complete (title, purpose, prompt, example result). Anonymize names and sensitive details in the example.

**Exercise 2:** No fixed result. Typical adaptations: tone (informal/formal address), length ("maximum 3 sentences"), format (table vs. list), fixed additions ("Ask me follow-up questions before you start"). The added value comes from making the prompt good once and then using it repeatably.

**Exercise 3:** Model solution:
```
I revise technical texts for an audience with no prior knowledge.
Task: Simplify the following text. Keep all key points,
remove technical terms or explain them in brackets.
Format: Continuous prose, at most twice the number of paragraphs as the original.
Constraints: Don't change the meaning; list your changes at the end.
### Text
[your invented technical text]
```
The comparison typically shows: the vague version delivers arbitrary cuts or style breaks; the structured version is repeatable and verifiable (list of changes).

**Exercise 4:** Example: (1) Is it correct? – the date of the club festival was wrong (the AI changed it) → corrected. (2) Am I allowed to share it? – contains real members' names → replaced with "member A/B"; labeling "with AI assistance" added. (3) My style? – too formal, informal address missing → tone changed to "you", sentences shortened. The check catches errors that are often missed by reading alone, because the text sounds so smooth.

**Exercise 5:** Example assessment: (1) AI no or only minimal – the relationship counts, your own words matter more. (2) AI yes – fixed workflow, easy to verify. (3) AI no – personal, sensitive, no routine. (4) AI yes – summary workflow. (5) AI yes – research preparation (questions rather than results). (6) AI no – concerns other people, high consequences, not verifiable. (7) AI yes – simple arithmetic (or a calculator). (8) AI with strong review – usable for structure and tone, but content and responsibility stay with you.
