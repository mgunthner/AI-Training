# Extra Exercises 05 – Prompt Basics

Cheatsheet: `01_cheatsheets/05_prompt_basics`

**Levels:** ★ Getting started · ★★ Everyday · ★★★ Challenge
**Rule:** Use only invented or anonymized data. All tasks work with any AI service and on a free plan. Suggested solutions are at the end. Only look once you're done.

---

## Task 1 ★ – Recognize the building blocks
**Scenario:** You see this prompt:

```
I'm inviting friends over for a barbecue on Saturday. Write a short
WhatsApp invitation. Tone: casual, use "you". At most 4 sentences,
no emojis.
```

**Task:** Find these building blocks in the prompt: goal/context, task, format, tone/audience, constraint. What's missing for the invitation to be directly usable?

**Hint:** Mark the building blocks with different colors or abbreviations.

---

## Task 2 ★ – From vague to concrete
**Scenario:** These three prompts are too vague:
- a) "Help me with my job application."
- b) "Plan something for Saturday."
- c) "Explain taxes to me."

**Task:** Pick two of them. For each, note three pieces of information the AI is missing, and rewrite the prompt.

**Hint:** Ask yourself: What do I need it for? For whom? What should the result look like? Use placeholders instead of real data.

---

## Task 3 ★★ – One task per prompt
**Scenario:** This prompt tries to do too much at once:

```
Write me an invitation for the parents' evening, translate it into English,
turn it into a social media post, and also plan the schedule
for the evening.
```

**Task:** Split the job into numbered steps. Test two variants and compare: (1) everything in one prompt, (2) step by step in several prompts.

**Hint:** Watch whether anything gets short-changed or goes missing in variant 1.

---

## Task 4 ★★ – Allow follow-up questions
**Scenario:** You're planning a weekend with friends but only have a rough idea.

**Task:**
1. Write a short prompt with your rough idea.
2. Append: "Ask me at most five follow-up questions before you start."
3. Answer the questions and have the plan created.
4. Present the same rough idea for comparison without the follow-up-question sentence.

**Hint:** Note which questions you hadn't thought of yourself.

---

## Task 5 ★★★ – Three rounds to a good prompt
**Scenario:** Your package arrived a week late. You want the AI to write a polite complaint (all details as placeholders: "Company X", "Date Y").

**Task:**
1. **Round 1:** Write a short, vague prompt.
2. **Round 2:** Add goal, context, format, tone, and limits.
3. **Round 3:** Read the result and give the AI two concrete improvement requests.
4. Rate each round from 1 to 5: Fits the goal? Fits the tone? Fits the length? Directly usable?

**Hint:** Save the best prompt with a title and purpose in your prompt library.

---

## Suggested solutions

**Task 1:** Goal/context: inviting friends over for a barbecue on Saturday. Task: write a short WhatsApp invitation. Format: at most 4 sentences. Tone/audience: casual, "you", friends. Constraint: no emojis. Missing: time, place, and what to bring. You can give these as placeholders or have the AI ask about them.

**Task 2:** Examples of what's missing: For a) field and industry, experience (briefly), whether a cover letter or résumé is meant. For b) who's coming, budget, indoors or outdoors. For c) which tax, for whom (employees, retirees …), how detailed. Rewritten, e.g.: "Explain in five sentences what income tax is. Audience: people starting their careers with no prior knowledge. Use an everyday example." For tax topics, verify the facts yourself.

**Task 3:** Possible observation: In the catch-all prompt, one part is often treated briefly or superficially. Step by step, you can check each result and improve it in a targeted way. Order: invitation, schedule, translation, post. Both variants are valid experiences. What matters is that you notice the difference.

**Task 4:** Typical follow-up questions: How many people? Budget? Travel? Dietary habits? With follow-up questions, the plan usually fits better because the AI doesn't have to guess. Questions you hadn't thought of yourself show the value of the sentence.

**Task 5:** Example for round 2:
```
Write a polite but firm complaint to Company X.
I ordered a package on Date Y. It arrived a week late.
I want an apology and a voucher.
Length: at most 120 words. Tone: factual, without accusations.
```
Typical: Round 1 is generic, round 2 is clearly more fitting, round 3 nails tone and length. The main lesson: Concrete details and revision beat a "perfect" first draft.
