# 03 – Model selection & free plans

**Definition:** A **model** is the "engine" behind the chat, the program that processes your request. There are larger and smaller models with different quality, speed and costs. A **plan** is your tariff with the provider (e.g. free or subscription).

> **Remember:** For a shopping list you don't need a truck, and for moving house you don't need a bicycle.

## Key points
- **Large vs. small:** Larger models are usually more thorough on tricky tasks, but slower, and they use up more of your limit. Smaller models are faster and good enough for routine tasks.
- **Standard vs. reasoning:** Reasoning models "think" internally before they answer. That helps with logic, planning and multi-step problems. More on this in topic 10.
- **Multimodal:** Some models process not only text but also images, audio and documents. Not every model can do everything.
- **Free plans often restrict:** access to certain models, usage volume, features (e.g. file upload, web search) and data use. The exact details change frequently.
- **Rule of thumb:** Simple task → small model. Tricky or important → larger model.
- **Bigger does not mean error-free:** Large models make things up too (topic 08).
- **Names and limits become outdated quickly:** check the current figures with your provider.

## Example
**Task 1:** You want the subject line of an email phrased in a friendlier way. → A small, fast model is enough.

**Task 2:** You are comparing two mobile plans and want to weigh up pros and cons. → Better use a larger model, and check the result yourself.

```
I have to choose between two mobile plans. Plan A: shorter contract term,
higher price. Plan B: longer term, cheaper price.
I mainly use my phone for messaging and navigation.
Compare both plans in a table and name the questions
I should clarify before signing up.
```

## Do / Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Choose the model to fit the task | Always take the largest model |
| Cross-check important results | Blindly trust a large model |
| Try a smaller model when you hit limits | Use reasoning mode for translating or rephrasing |

## Safety note
On the free plan, inputs can be stored and, depending on provider or settings, used for training – no matter which model you choose. Check your privacy settings (topic 04).

## Mini checklist
- [ ] I know the difference between large and small models.
- [ ] I know when I need a larger model or reasoning mode.
- [ ] I know what my free plan restricts and have checked that with the provider.
- [ ] I check important results, no matter which model produced them.

## Exercise (5 min.)
Solve the same task with two models, modes (standard and reasoning mode) or services.

```
Plan three days in a city of your choice for two people on a small budget. Give a plan for each day plus a rainy-day alternative.
```

Compare: quality, speed, comprehensibility. Note your conclusion in your prompt library.
