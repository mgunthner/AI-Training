# 22 – Legal & compliance

**Definition:** **Legal & compliance** covers the ground rules that apply when using AI in everyday life: data protection (GDPR), copyright, labeling requirements and the responsibility that stays with you.

> **Remember:** The AI does the work, the responsibility stays with you.

**Important:** This cheatsheet is not legal advice. It offers orientation for everyday use. The legal situation keeps evolving; if in doubt, ask an expert or the data protection officer of your organization.

## Key points
- **GDPR (data protection, German: DSGVO):** **Personal data** is anything that can be linked to a person (name, address, photos, often also email addresses). **Rule:** other people's personal data does not belong in an AI chat, especially on free plans, where inputs may be used for training (topic 04). **Data processing on behalf of others** (German legal term: "Auftragsverarbeitung") means: anyone who processes data on behalf of an organization needs a contractual basis for doing so – for work devices and work data, the employer's rules apply.
- **Copyright:** Uploading **your own content** (texts, images) can affect the rights to it – check who owns them and what the service's terms of use say. Don't upload **other people's content** (e.g. protected texts or photos) without permission. **AI output:** whether and for what purposes you may use AI-generated output under intellectual property law is partly unresolved – be careful with important works (a book, a logo) and get expert advice.
- **Labeling:** EU rules require transparency for certain AI applications (e.g. AI-generated images, chatbots). Platform and employer rules also apply. **Practical rule:** if the origin is not clearly recognizable, label the AI-generated parts.
- **Responsibility:** The AI can make mistakes (topic 08). If you publish or send something, you are responsible for it – "the AI wrote it" is no excuse.
- **Exams, school, university:** Every institution has its own rules about where and how AI may be used. Those rules apply, even if the service itself allows it.

## Example
You want to evaluate a members list for your club (fictional: "Example Club"):

| Step | Legal aspect | Safe way |
|---|---|---|
| Load the members list into the tool | other people's personal data (names, addresses) | Anonymize the list: "Member A", "Member B" (topic 04) |
| Have statistics created | no personal individual data needed | Only have aggregated numbers evaluated |
| Use the result in the newsletter | labeling for AI-generated text parts | Briefly mark it: "Created with AI assistance" |
| Keep the members list in the chat | the history may be stored | Delete the chat after the task, check the training usage |

The evaluation works just as well with anonymized data – without legal risk for you or the club.

## Do / Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Anonymize other people's personal data | Dump address or customer lists into the chat |
| Label AI-generated parts where it isn't obvious | Pass AI output off as entirely your own work |
| Check your employer's and institution's rules | Use private AI accounts for company data |
| Ask an expert when unsure | "Settle" legal questions with a chatbot |

## Safety note
No personal data, nothing confidential, no third-party copyrighted content in the chat – this applies especially to free plans. Your employer's rules always take precedence.

## Mini checklist
- [ ] I can explain what personal data is.
- [ ] I don't upload other people's data without anonymizing it.
- [ ] I know the AI rules of my institution and my employer.
- [ ] I know that responsibility for the output stays with me.
- [ ] When legal questions come up, I get expert advice instead of asking the AI.
