## Topic 22 – Legal & Compliance
Cheatsheet: `01_cheatsheets/22_legal_compliance`

**Question 1 – B (1 P):** Name, date of birth and address clearly identify a person. A, C and D are anonymous statistics or conditions.

**Question 2 – False (1 P):** Responsibility stays with the human who uses and sends the output. "The AI wrote it" doesn't let anyone off the hook.

**Question 3 – Model answer (2 P):**
(a) 1 P for at least three pieces of information, e.g. name, address, phone number, invoice number (identifying in context), health information (knee surgery) – especially sensitive.
(b) 1 P, e.g.:
```
From: Person A
Dear Team, I received a defective coffee machine on 5 March
(invoice 2026-4471). Please reply promptly.
```
Removed: name, address, phone number, health information. Kept: date, invoice number, core message – the analysis remains possible. (Alternatively, also shorten the invoice number if the analysis doesn't need it.)

**Question 4 – B (1 P):** The organization's rules (the club) apply in addition to platform requirements; if in doubt, label it. A conceals it, C is inadmissible, D is excessive.

**Question 5 – False (1 P):** Exams are governed by the rules of the institution (school, university, employer) – they can be stricter than the service's terms of service. Both apply side by side.

## Topic 23 – Everyday Workflows & Prompt Library
Cheatsheet: `01_cheatsheets/23_everyday_workflows`

**Question 6 – A (1 P):** Title, purpose, prompt, anonymized sample result (plus, if applicable, a note on model/mode). B is too thin, C violates the data rule (topics 04, 22), D has no place in a note file.

**Question 7 – Model answer (2 P):**
```
Title: Executive Summary
Purpose: Turn long texts quickly into what matters for decisions.
Prompt: Summarize the following text in five bullet points.
Target audience: executive without specialist knowledge. Format:
maximum 5 bullet points, each at most 2 lines. No introduction,
no evaluation. If a key piece of information is missing, mark
it with [OPEN].
### Text
[Text]
Sample result: 5 bullet points from a made-up project report.
```
- Purpose (1 P): describes the recurring problem the prompt solves.
- Prompt (1 P): names the target audience and format specification; bonus (no points): limits such as "no evaluation" or the [OPEN] marker.

**Question 8 – B (1 P):** A library is a living document: rework, test, update the entry. Deleting (A) wastes work, filler words (C) usually make things worse, switching services (D) doesn't solve a prompt problem.

## Topic 24 – Glossary & Further Learning
Cheatsheet: `01_cheatsheets/24_glossary_further_learning`

**Question 9 – Model answer (2 P):**
(a) 1 P, e.g.: "A hallucination is an invented but convincing-sounding AI answer – for example made-up sources, numbers or quotes."
(b) 1 P, e.g.: "Human-in-the-loop means: the human approves actions with consequences (sending, deleting, paying) before they are executed."
Point allocation: correct core, understandable language, one sentence.

**Question 10 – A, B, C, D (2 P):** Benefit, risk, costs and data privacy are the check questions of the new-feature check (plus undo). Social hype (E) is precisely not a quality criterion.

**Question 11 – B (1 P):** Official documentation and help pages are the most reliable source for features, limits and prices – they change often. Social media (A) and old forums (D) are outdated or promotional, and the AI itself (C) doesn't reliably know its current limits.

**Question 12 – False (1 P):** Test new features first with made-up data – if in doubt, in a test environment. Using real data immediately is exactly the risk the safety rules (topics 04, 21) are meant to avoid.
